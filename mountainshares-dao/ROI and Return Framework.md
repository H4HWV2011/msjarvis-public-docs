# MountainShares ROI and Return Framework

**Status:** Final Draft  
**Applies to:** MountainShares-related participation, operations, community-value measurement, and any future private-capital discussion  
**Does not apply to:** A current securities offering, promise of investment return, charitable solicitation, or binding distribution agreement

## 1. Purpose

MountainShares is being developed to test whether locally grounded digital infrastructure can create useful, measurable, and locally accountable value.

That value may take more than one form:

- Financial sustainability for lawful operating entities and professional services
- Potential participant or member benefit under approved surplus-distribution rules
- Local economic circulation through commerce, services, and relationships
- Public benefit through community information, heritage, education, stewardship, access, and participation
- Technical and organizational capacity that remains connected to the communities creating it

This document establishes how MountainShares will distinguish those forms of value from one another. Its purpose is to prevent vague claims, protect participants, support transparent governance, and ensure that no one mistakes community benefit or charitable support for a guaranteed financial investment return.

## 2. Plain-language statement

MountainShares is not currently offering a guaranteed financial return to investors.

Any future payment, distribution, repayment, patronage-style allocation, revenue share, interest payment, equity appreciation, or other economic benefit must be:

1. Authorized by the appropriate legal entity and governing documents
2. Supported by actual revenues, verified financial records, and available cash
3. Made only after applicable expenses, obligations, taxes, maintenance needs, and reserve requirements have been addressed
4. Clearly described in writing before funds are accepted or rights are issued
5. Subject to applicable law, legal review, tax treatment, and risk disclosure

No individual should contribute money, labor, data, property, or other value based on an expectation of financial return unless the terms of that expectation are specifically documented and legally valid.

## 3. Four forms of return

### 3.1 Financial return

Financial return means money or monetizable value received by a person or entity in connection with capital provided to a legally defined operating entity.

Examples may include:

- Repayment of principal under a promissory note
- Interest under a lawful lending arrangement
- Revenue-share payments under a written agreement
- Dividends or distributions associated with a valid ownership interest
- Proceeds from a sale, redemption, or transfer of an ownership interest

Financial return is not assumed. It must be defined by written terms.

### 3.2 Participant or member surplus benefit

Participant or member surplus benefit means a conditional allocation of verified surplus under approved MountainShares governance and distribution rules.

This may include a distribution, credit, local-use benefit, patronage-style allocation, or another lawful form of recognition. Eligibility, calculation methods, timing, payment form, caps, reserves, and approval requirements must be defined in the governing documents.

A participant or member surplus benefit is not a guaranteed investment yield.

### 3.3 Operating return

Operating return means the system's ability to sustain its work through earned revenue and prudent financial management.

Examples include:

- Revenue sufficient to support platform operations
- Maintenance and replacement capacity
- Appropriate technical support
- Community operating capacity
- Reserve funding
- Compliance, insurance, accounting, and administrative needs
- Compensation for contracted or professional work performed by the appropriate entity

Operating return should be evaluated before discretionary surplus distribution.

### 3.4 Community and public-benefit return

Community and public-benefit return means measurable value created for people and places, whether or not that value is paid as cash to an individual.

Examples may include:

- Local transactions and local-value circulation
- Merchant, producer, worker, and service-provider participation
- Access to useful public information
- Heritage interpretation and cultural contribution
- Volunteer contribution and community stewardship
- Inclusion of people and communities often left out of digital systems
- Improved access to local resources, opportunities, safety information, or community notices
- Transparent governance and demonstrable safeguards

Community benefit must not be represented as a private investor profit or financial yield.

## 4. Financial ROI calculation

When, and only when, MountainShares or an affiliated operating entity has a valid documented capital instrument, realized financial return may be calculated as:

\[
\text{Realized ROI} =
\frac{\text{Total cash and monetizable proceeds received} - \text{Capital contributed}}{\text{Capital contributed}} \times 100
\]

For example, if a person lawfully contributes $10,000 to a defined instrument and later receives $12,500 in total principal repayment, interest, distributions, or sale proceeds:

\[
\text{Realized ROI} =
\frac{\$12{,}500 - \$10{,}000}{\$10{,}000} \times 100 = 25\%
\]

This is a retrospective calculation of what actually occurred. It is not a forecast or promise.

## 5. Annualized return

When a capital instrument has a defined time period, annualized return may be calculated as:

\[
\text{Annualized return} =
\left(
\frac{\text{Ending value}}{\text{Beginning capital}}
\right)^{\frac{1}{\text{Years held}}}
- 1
\]

Annualized return must state:

- The measurement period
- Whether the figure is gross or net of fees, taxes, and expenses
- Whether values are realized cash payments or estimated paper values
- The assumptions used
- The risks and limitations affecting the calculation

## 6. Preconditions for surplus distribution

No surplus should be treated as distributable until the responsible entity has verified that the following conditions have been met:

1. Revenue and expenses have been recorded and reviewed
2. Required operating obligations have been paid or properly reserved
3. Taxes, insurance, compliance costs, and contractual obligations have been addressed
4. Maintenance, replacement, cybersecurity, and contingency reserves have been funded according to policy
5. Restricted grants, charitable funds, donated assets, and purpose-limited funds have been properly segregated
6. The applicable governing body has reviewed the available surplus
7. Distribution eligibility and calculation rules have been applied consistently
8. The distribution complies with governing documents and applicable law
9. The distribution does not impair the system's ability to meet ongoing obligations
10. The action has been documented for transparent reporting

The Phase 3 Surplus Distribution Specification should govern the specific conditions, allocation rules, and approval procedures applicable to MountainShares surplus.

## 7. No commingling of return categories

The following categories must remain distinct in financial records, governance materials, public communications, and participant disclosures:

| Source or structure | Primary purpose | May it be described as investor ROI? |
|---|---|---|
| Harmony for Hope charitable gifts | Charitable public benefit, stewardship, education, heritage, and community participation | No |
| Grant funding | Restricted or unrestricted support for approved public-purpose work and deliverables | No |
| Sponsorship support | Support in exchange for defined acknowledgment or permissible benefits | No, unless a separate lawful capital instrument exists |
| KTS professional-services revenue | Payment for technical development, consulting, support, or other contracted work | No |
| MountainShares participant benefit | Conditional benefit or surplus allocation under approved rules | Not unless the governing instrument explicitly creates an investor right |
| Private-capital instrument | A legally defined investment, loan, ownership interest, or revenue-share agreement | Potentially, if formally documented and compliant |

Volunteer labor, donated goods, donated professional services, charitable gifts, grants, and restricted public funds must never be used to inflate, imply, or guarantee an investor return.

## 8. Required disclosures for any future capital opportunity

Before any person is invited to provide private capital in expectation of financial return, the responsible entity must provide written information that addresses:

- The legal name and structure of the entity receiving funds
- The purpose and permitted use of funds
- The amount being sought and any minimum or maximum raise
- The type of instrument being offered
- The rights, obligations, and limitations of the capital provider
- Whether there is ownership, voting power, repayment priority, revenue participation, or conversion rights
- The projected revenue model and principal assumptions
- Material operating, financial, legal, regulatory, technology, market, and liquidity risks
- Whether repayment or distributions are guaranteed; absent an enforceable written guarantee, they must be described as not guaranteed
- Reserve requirements and payment priority
- Fees, compensation, conflicts of interest, and related-party relationships
- Reporting frequency and the method for communicating changes
- Transfer, redemption, maturity, default, and exit provisions
- Applicable tax considerations and the need for independent legal, financial, and tax advice

No public language should imply a specific percentage return before these disclosures, supporting forecasts, and appropriate legal review exist.

## 9. Return reporting dashboard

MountainShares should report performance in separate categories rather than collapsing all value into a single number.

### Financial sustainability

- Gross earned revenue
- Operating expense
- Net operating result
- Cash on hand
- Reserve balance
- Maintenance and replacement obligations
- Unrestricted and restricted funds
- Debt or repayment obligations, if any

### Participant and surplus measures

- Verified distributable surplus, if any
- Amount retained for reserves
- Amount allocated to approved community purposes
- Amount allocated to eligible participants, if any
- Number and share of eligible recipients
- Average and median distribution, where disclosure is appropriate
- Concentration of distributions
- Appeals, corrections, or exceptions

### Local-circulation measures

- Total local transaction volume
- Number of participating merchants, workers, producers, and households
- Estimated share of activity retained locally
- Repeat participation
- Geographic distribution of participation
- Categories of goods, services, and needs being met

### Community and public-benefit measures

- Access to public information and local resources
- Heritage, education, and stewardship engagement
- Volunteer and community contribution, reported responsibly
- Inclusion and accessibility indicators
- Community feedback, complaints, corrections, and response time
- Safeguard performance and incident reporting

## 10. Forecasting policy

Forecasts may be developed for internal planning, grant applications, responsible fundraising, and future capital discussions. Forecasts must never be presented as guaranteed outcomes.

Every forecast must identify:

- The base date and projection period
- Revenue assumptions by source
- Adoption and participation assumptions
- Pricing assumptions
- Direct and indirect costs
- Staffing, technical, legal, insurance, and maintenance assumptions
- Reserve policy
- Best-case, base-case, and downside-case scenarios
- Key dependencies, including funding, permits, partnerships, technical performance, and public participation
- The entity to which each revenue stream and expense belongs
- Whether figures are preliminary, reviewed, audited, or independently validated

## 11. Governance and conflicts

Any person or entity with a financial interest in KTS, MountainShares operations, technical infrastructure, vendor contracts, capital arrangements, or surplus distributions must disclose that interest to the appropriate governing body.

Conflicts must be documented and managed through written procedures. Individuals with a material conflict should not control decisions about their own compensation, contract award, capital repayment, or distribution eligibility.

Governance must preserve clear boundaries among:

- Harmony for Hope, Inc., as the charitable and public-benefit anchor
- MountainShares, as the developing participation, local-value, and governance framework
- Kidd's Technical Services, as the technical-development and professional-services provider
- Any future legally distinct operating, cooperative, membership, lending, or investment entity

## 12. Public claims standard

MountainShares communications should use clear language.

Appropriate statements include:

- "MountainShares is developing a framework for measurable local value and responsible community participation."
- "Any future surplus distribution would depend on verified results, reserve requirements, eligibility rules, governance approval, and applicable law."
- "Community benefit is not the same as a guaranteed financial return."
- "No investment return is promised."
- "Financial projections, if later developed, will be assumption-based and subject to risk."

Statements to avoid unless supported by formally reviewed documents include:

- "Guaranteed ROI"
- "Risk-free return"
- "Passive income"
- "Invest now and earn"
- "Your contribution will multiply"
- "Community impact equals financial return"
- "Grant funding guarantees investor repayment"
- "Volunteer labor makes the investment profitable"

## 13. Review and revision

This framework should be reviewed before:

- Accepting capital that carries an expectation of financial return
- Announcing or calculating a participant surplus distribution
- Publishing financial projections for external reliance
- Entering a material related-party transaction
- Changing reserve, allocation, or governance rules
- Launching a new operating entity, membership structure, revenue-share arrangement, or investment instrument

This document is a transparency and governance framework. It does not replace legal counsel, tax advice, accounting review, securities-law analysis, nonprofit compliance review, or fiduciary duties.
