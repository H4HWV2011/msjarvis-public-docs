# ALLIS — Auditable Observer-Dependence

**Document classification:** Foundational Vision and Governance Requirement  
**System:** Administrative Learning and Location Intelligence System (ALLIS)  
**Research alignment:** Meaning Requires Geometry  
**Status:** Proposed architectural requirement — not yet implemented or qualified  
**Date:** November 3, 2026

## 1. Vision

ALLIS shall support different human perspectives without silently altering the evidentiary foundations upon which conclusions are formed.

Users may differ in geographic location, economic circumstances, institutional relationships, personal histories, and information needs. These differences may legitimately affect the relevance and presentation of information.

They must not become undisclosed mechanisms for manipulating beliefs, suppressing credible evidence, or advancing institutional political preferences.

**Foundational principle:**

> Observer-dependence must be something we can audit, rather than something we merely hope to avoid.

## 2. Architectural intent

ALLIS shall distinguish between:

- **Observer context:** Information legitimately needed to understand a user's question.
- **Evidence:** Verifiable information supporting factual conclusions.
- **Interpretation:** Reasoning conducted from the available evidence.
- **Institutional position:** An organization's expressed interests, priorities, or advocacy.
- **Governance:** Rules determining authorization, permissible personalization, evidence handling, and accountability.

Personalization may change presentation and relevant context. It must not secretly change evidentiary standards or select political arguments to produce preferred beliefs.

## 3. Relationship to Meaning Requires Geometry

This requirement establishes a research question concerning observer-dependent representations and observer-invariant properties.

When an observer's context changes, which factual, relational, and evidentiary properties must remain preserved for an inference to remain warranted?

The research shall distinguish legitimate contextual variation from changes that introduce bias, omit material evidence, or distort factual relationships.

Analogies with observer-dependent coordinate descriptions in relativity provide conceptual motivation, not proof of mathematical or physical equivalence.

## 4. Future architectural requirements

ALLIS should support:

1. Separation of verified evidence from user preferences and institutional advocacy.
2. Explicit governance of how personal memory influences answer generation.
3. Traceability of source selection, retrieval, attribution, and material uncertainties.
4. Counterfactual testing across synthetic observers and institutional contexts.
5. Longitudinal evaluation of cumulative political reinforcement and selective framing.
6. Detection of changes in factual conclusions caused by irrelevant observer characteristics.
7. Human review, correction, and accountable governance of identified failures.

Auditing must respect privacy, consent, authorized access, and retention limitations.

## 5. Development boundary

This document does not authorize modifications to the active H_people production-closure implementation.

It establishes a requirement for future architectural review and qualification. Implementation mechanisms shall be selected only after the existing governed conversation pathway is reliably operating and reproducibly tested.

## 6. Acceptance principle

Before ALLIS is qualified for public-facing civic or electoral information services, controlled tests must demonstrate that changes in irrelevant personal history, inferred political affiliation, or institutional preference do not systematically alter core verified facts, evidentiary standards, or material uncertainty.

Legitimate differences in geographic context and user-requested explanations must remain permissible.

Test methods, pass/fail criteria, and acceptable variation shall be specified in a separate research protocol.

**Verification status:** Not yet tested.

---

**Governing statement**

ALLIS may adapt her explanations to the observer. She must not covertly adapt the evidence to influence the observer.

Observer-dependence shall be identified, measured, and subject to independent audit.
