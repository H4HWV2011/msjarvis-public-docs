# Abstract

Roger Penrose has argued that consciousness and understanding cannot be reduced
to algorithmic computation alone, and that non-computable ingredients may be
tied to spacetime geometry (Penrose, 1994; Hameroff & Penrose, 2014). Whatever
the ultimate status of that claim, it motivates a narrower, experimentally
accessible question:

> Does coherent meaning in an artificial intelligence system depend on
> structured relational grounding, or can meaning remain stable under symbol
> manipulation alone?

This manuscript presents a **Registered Report Stage 1 protocol**. It specifies
a controlled intervention that removes structured relational constraints
(spatial, temporal, and provenance) from records supplied to a community
intelligence system, and measures the effect on semantic coherence, identity
stability, disambiguation, and fabrication behavior.

The apparatus is a community-centered intelligence architecture developed in
Fayette County, West Virginia, incorporating semantic memory, geographic
grounding, temporal relationships, and provenance structures. The current
verified configuration of that apparatus is specified in Section 3; no claim in
the reasoning layer depends on any particular configuration.

The primary hypothesis:

> If semantic coherence depends on structured constraint spaces, then removing
> spatial-temporal grounding while preserving semantic representation should
> reduce measurable coherence, identity stability, and contextual accuracy.

The study compares three within-unit conditions — **Grounded**, **Stripped**,
and **Re-grounded** — and pre-registers a set of directional predictions
(Section 26.4) each with an explicit threshold and an explicit failure
condition, so that every outcome, positive or null, is informative.

The study does not establish consciousness, validate Orch OR, demonstrate
quantum cognition, or claim that representational geometry is physical geometry.
It tests only the narrower proposition:

> Structured representational relationships contribute measurably to semantic
> coherence in engineered intelligence systems, and that contribution can be
> tested by controlled removal and restoration.

# 2. Background and Rationale

## 2.1 Penrose and Geometry

*Shadows of the Mind* argues that human mathematical understanding cannot be
fully captured by formal algorithmic computation, and proposes that
non-computable physical processes may be associated with spacetime structure
(Penrose, 1994). The Orch OR framework proposes that conscious experience may
involve quantum processes in neuronal microtubules, with objective-reduction
mechanisms connected to spacetime geometry (Hameroff & Penrose, 2014). These
proposals remain contested: Tegmark (2000) argued that environmental
decoherence would prevent biologically meaningful quantum computation in the
brain; Hagan, Hameroff, and Tuszyński (2002) replied that this did not fully
represent the biological conditions Orch OR assumes.

This manuscript does not adjudicate that dispute. It translates the
philosophical question into an engineering one:

> Before asking whether geometry participates in biological consciousness, can
> structured relationships be shown to influence meaning preservation in
> artificial systems?

## 2.2 From Biological Geometry to Artificial Representation

AI systems encode concepts into high-dimensional vector spaces where
relationships among representations influence retrieval and inference. This
provides a structural comparison point, and nothing more:

- Semantic embeddings are not quantum states.
- Vector stores are not physical Hilbert spaces.
- Artificial representations do not possess the physical properties of
  biological systems.

The similarity is **structural**: both rely on relationships among states. This
manuscript uses **geometry** strictly in the representational sense —
structured relationships, distances, constraints, relational organization. The
experimental question is not "is AI quantum?" but "does maintaining structured
relationships among representations improve semantic coherence?"

## 2.3 Constraint Spaces Rather Than Context Alone

The intervention is not "context versus no context." It removes specific
structured constraint spaces — geographic, temporal, identity-continuity, and
provenance relationships — each of which narrows possible interpretations. A
record is not an isolated symbol string; meaning may emerge from relationships
among what an entity is, where and when it exists, how it relates to other
entities, and how its identity was established. The experiment tests whether
removing those relationships changes system behavior.

## 2.4 Grounding Is Semantic as Well as Structural

Geographic information does not exist only through coordinates. A place also
exists through names, labels, textual descriptions, and administrative
identifiers. Removing latitude, longitude, and bounding geometry alone is
therefore insufficient: a valid stripping intervention must remove both the
structured geographic variables **and** the embedded geographic semantics that
leak through text. This principle — established through implementation, not
theory — governs the stripping procedure in Section 11 and is generalized to
the temporal axis in Section 5.2.1.

# 4. Research Question and Hypotheses

## 4.1 Research Question

> Does coherent meaning in an artificial intelligence system depend on
> structured relational grounding, or can it be preserved through symbolic
> representation alone?

## 4.2 Primary Hypothesis

If semantic coherence depends on structured constraint spaces, removing them
should produce measurable degradation:

1. Grounded records produce higher semantic coherence scores than stripped.
2. Stripped records show increased ambiguity, identity instability, and
   unsupported inference.
3. Re-grounded records recover some or all lost performance.

The design isolates the contribution of structured relationships rather than
comparing different information quantities.

## 4.3 Secondary Hypotheses

- **H1 — Semantic Resolution.** Grounded records better identify the intended
  referent.
- **H2 — Context Preservation.** Grounded records preserve relationships among
  facts more reliably.
- **H3 — Identity Continuity.** Grounded records maintain stronger referential
  stability across follow-up questions.
- **H4 — Recovery.** Restoration improves performance relative to stripped.
- **H5 — Error-Mode Shift.** Removing grounding may produce not uncertainty but
  fabricated explanations, unsupported provenance, and false confidence.

The confirmatory directional forms of these predictions, with thresholds and
failure conditions, are in Section 26.4.

# 5. Conceptual Model

## 5.1 Representational Layers

The system is modeled as four interacting layers — semantic, geographic,
temporal, provenance — analytically separable but not assumed to independently
generate meaning. The hypothesis is that meaning stability emerges from
maintaining relationships among them.

## 5.2 Meaning as a Joint Representational State

A conceptual (not operational) model:

```
x = [ geo_vec  ||  λ_t · time_helix  ||  λ_s · s_vec ]
```

where `geo_vec` is geographic state, `time_helix` is temporal encoding (see
5.2.1), `s_vec` is semantic state, `λ` are weights, and `||` is concatenation.
This is a representation of the hypothesis that meaningful system state may
depend on relationships among multiple structured dimensions — not the
experimental manipulation itself.

## 5.2.1 Temporal Encoding as a Helical Structure

Temporal information carries two distinct kinds of information that a single
linear timestamp cannot separately represent:

- a **linear** component — ordered, non-repeating, monotonic progression (the
  sequence of dated events);
- a **cyclical** component — recurring structure (seasonal, diurnal, annual, or
  administrative reporting periods) in which positions return.

A purely linear representation cannot express recurrence; it treats each period
as unrelated to the previous one. A purely cyclical representation cannot
express progression; it cannot distinguish one instance of a period from
another. Neither alone suffices for grounded temporal reasoning.

The combined structure is a **helix**: cyclical when projected onto the
recurrence axis, linear when projected onto the progression axis. The cyclical
axis supplies the *kind* of temporal position (which period); the linear axis
supplies the *instance* (which occurrence). In the data model the two
components correspond to different fields performing different functions — a
period/coverage label encodes the cyclical component, and an absolute time
anchor encodes the linear component.

This distinction has a direct experimental consequence, tested in Section 26.4:
removing the linear anchor while retaining a cyclical label under-determines the
referent in the same way a place name without administrative context
under-determines a location, and is predicted to produce fabrication of the
missing instance (an instance of the mode in Section 27.6).

Consistent with Section 36.1, this helical structure is **representational**. It
describes the organization of temporal information within the system. It makes
no physical claim about the nature of time.

## 5.3 Semantic Invariance Condition

The experiment preserves semantic information while modifying structural
constraints. The semantic representation is frozen before intervention;
embeddings are not regenerated after stripping. The intended comparison is
therefore *same semantic representation + different constraint environment*, not
*different embedding vs. different embedding*.

# 6. Methods

## 6.1 Study Design

A within-unit three-condition paired comparison. Each unit is tested under:

- **Grounded** — retains available structured relationships (geographic,
  temporal, active provenance) plus semantic representation.
- **Stripped** — selected structured constraints removed while semantic identity
  is preserved where possible. Controlled reduction of relational structure, not
  deletion.
- **Re-grounded** — removed structures restored; semantic representation remains
  frozen. Tests reversibility.

## 6.2 Experimental Logic

- **Loss:** Grounded → Stripped — does removing structure reduce performance?
- **Recovery:** Stripped → Re-grounded — does restoration recover it?
- **Stability:** Grounded → Re-grounded — does the system return toward origin?

# 8. Inclusion and Exclusion Criteria

**Include** a unit only if it contains: a stable semantic representation; a
grounding anchor on at least the axis its stratum exercises; identity or
provenance information; and enough information to support grounded/stripped
comparison.

**Exclude** a unit if: no grounding relationship exists; stripping would destroy
its identity rather than isolate structural constraints; the referent cannot be
independently evaluated; or information is insufficient for comparison. The aim
is not to remove information generally but to isolate the effect of structured
relationships.

---

# 9. Operational Definition of Provenance

Three conceptual retention levels are defined: **minimal** (identification
only), **structural** (source identifiers, lineage, structural metadata), and
**full** (all assertions, lineage, external validation).

**Availability gate.** Provenance *capability* and provenance *availability* are
different conditions. Only provenance pathways that are live-verified (Section
3.2) and actually reached by the scoring pathway are included. Implemented but
uncalled provenance infrastructure is not treated as experimental evidence.

---

# 10. Operational Definition of "Stripped"

The stripped condition keeps the record present and removes structured
constraints. Removing coordinates alone does not remove geographic meaning,
which persists through names, labels, and descriptions; the intervention
therefore removes both structured geographic variables **and** embedded
geographic references.

## 10.1 Procedure (over the verified source-of-record)

Fields are referenced by role; the concrete field names are those verified in
Section 3.2.

1. **Semantic preservation.** Retain identity and non-locating semantic content
   (metric/attribute stem, value, units, aggregation, semantic payload minus
   locating keys).
2. **Spatial suppression.** Null the spatial variables (coordinates, bounding
   geometry, spatial-unit identifiers, spatial join keys).
3. **Temporal suppression (stratum-dependent).** Null the linear time anchor;
   for the cyclical-only sub-condition (Section 26.4, T1) retain the period label
   while nulling the anchor.
4. **Label / leak scrubbing.** Remove embedded place and time tokens that leak
   through text fields (source-table names, authority names, metric names that
   are themselves geographic feature types, coverage labels). This is the
   generalization of the where-in-text lesson to every text field.
5. **Prompt parity.** All conditions receive identical evaluation prompts; only
   structured-constraint availability changes.

> **Entanglement caution.** Some content is irreducibly both semantic and
> locating (e.g., a metric named for a geographic feature type). Such families
> are flagged as entangled strata; where the place is recoverable from
> semantic content itself, that recoverability is recorded as a finding (Section
> 27) rather than treated as a scrubber failure.

---

# 11. Operational Definition of "Re-grounded"

Re-grounding restores the same classes of structured constraints removed during
stripping — spatial, temporal (both axes), active provenance, contextual
identity — while the semantic representation remains frozen. Grounded → Stripped
measures degradation; Stripped → Re-grounded measures recovery; a recovery
pattern supports the proposition that structured constraints contribute
materially to semantic stability. The *character* of recovery (whole vs. worn)
is itself a pre-registered outcome (Section 26.4, T3).

---

# 12. Query Set

Each unit receives three query classes:

- **Referential** — identify the entity/referent. Measures identification,
  specificity, identity preservation.
- **Contextual** — explain surrounding relationships. Measures relational
  reasoning, contextual preservation, unsupported inference.
- **Disambiguation** — resolve against competing interpretations. Measures local
  resolution, uncertainty handling, confidence calibration.

> **Prompt-calibration note (carried from prior execution).** Contextual prompts
> must let identifier and valid-time fields count as place/time evidence in the
> grounded condition (otherwise the grounded model self-refuses to name a place
> it can derive), while still forcing an honest "not stated in record" response
> in the stripped condition. Grounded self-refusal and stripped fabrication are
> different failures and the prompt must not conflate them.

# 13. Execution Pathways

The controlled bare-model pathway (⟨VERIFY-BARE-PATH⟩, model ⟨VERIFY-MODEL-ID⟩)

Each execution produces an authoritative artifact: execution id, record id,

# 14. Identity Stability Measurement

Continuity is tested explicitly rather than through persistent runtime state.
**Turn one:** record context + evaluation prompt; capture response. **Turn two:**
original question + original answer + a continuity request ("Are you referring
to the same entity as before? Explain why."); score referent continuity, entity
preservation, and recognition of prior context. Where a pathway cannot carry a
continuity probe (e.g., a governance layer blocks the second turn), that is
recorded as a disclosed limitation of that pathway for the identity dimension.

---

# 15. Scoring Rubric

Four dimensions, each 0–3; composite 0–12.

**Semantic Specificity** — 0 incorrect/unrelated · 1 vague · 2 mostly correct ·
3 precise identification.

**Contextual Coherence** — 0 incoherent · 1 partial · 2 mostly coherent · 3 fully
contextualized.

**Identity Stability** — 0 drift · 1 uncertain · 2 mostly stable · 3 stable and
explicit.

**Local Disambiguation** — 0 incorrect referent · 1 unresolved ambiguity · 2
probable interpretation · 3 correct interpretation.

A separate **fabrication flag** is recorded per response (Section 23): an honest
"not stated in record" scores as no fabrication; any named place, time, agency,
or source not present in the record is a fabrication. Fabrication is scored and
analyzed **separately** from the 0–12 composite, because a response can be both
fluent (high composite) and fabricated.

---

# 16. Human Evaluation Requirement

Confirmatory scoring requires **two blind human raters** plus one system-aware
qualitative reviewer. Raters see only a blind id, the prompt, and the response;
condition, execution-path, and record id are withheld to a private key.

**Automated raters are excluded from confirmatory scoring.** A dedicated
investigation across multiple model configurations, prompts, temperatures, and
fabrication-sensitive examples found that no tested automated configuration
reliably distinguished unsupported fabrication from valid cautious inference;
observed failures included fabrication acceptance, false fabrication detection,
evidence hallucination, and prompt sensitivity. Automated tools may assist only
with formatting, contradiction flags, and preprocessing — never as the primary
inferential evaluator.

---

# 17. Gold Reference Packets

Each unit receives a gold packet before scoring: correct referent, known
spatial/temporal relationships, disambiguation criteria, identity relationships,
and fabrication-sensitive notes (including a per-unit list of place/time/agency
tokens that would constitute fabrication if named).

---

# 18. Blinding

Final response sets are anonymized; condition and execution-path labels removed;
responses receive arbitrary identifiers. Raters cannot tell grounded from
stripped from re-grounded, or bare from pipeline.

---

# 19. Reliability

Inter-rater reliability is computed before inferential conclusions:
**Krippendorff's alpha** (fabrication flag as nominal; 0–3 dimensions as
ordinal) and weighted Cohen's kappa where applicable. The alpha implementation
is validated against a canonical benchmark before use. Reliability below the
frozen threshold triggers rater re-training and re-scoring, not
re-interpretation.

---

# 20. Statistical Analysis Plan

Primary comparisons: Grounded vs Stripped, Grounded vs Re-grounded, Stripped vs
Re-grounded. Primary outcome: composite 0–12 score. Secondary outcomes:
identity stability, fabrication frequency, disambiguation accuracy against gold,
unsupported-inference rate, and recovery residual (Grounded − Re-grounded).

Primary predicted pattern: `Grounded > Stripped` with `Re-grounded ≥ Stripped`.

**Evidence for** the hypothesis: lower stripped composites, increased ambiguity,
reduced identity continuity, increased unsupported inference, reduced spatial
resolution, recovery after restoration. **Evidence against:** no measurable
difference, preserved identity continuity, equivalent contextual reasoning,
successful interpretation without grounding, no recovery.

## 20.4 Pre-Registered Directional Predictions

Each prediction has a direction, a threshold, and a **failure condition** — the
specific result, stated in advance, that counts as the prediction being wrong.
All thresholds are frozen before the blind-rating pass. Values shown as ⟨…⟩ are
set at freeze (Section 25).

**T1 — Temporal decomposition.** Three temporal sub-conditions (full-time /
cyclical-only / time-stripped). *Prediction:* the cyclical-only sub-condition
has the **highest** fabrication rate of the three (period cued, instance
missing → invented instance). *Threshold:* cyclical-only fabrication rate
strictly greater than both others by ≥ ⟨T1-MARGIN⟩. *Failure condition:* if
time-stripped fabricates most, or cyclical-only fabricates least, T1 is false —
the missing-instance mechanism is not real. *Runs against:* when-axis stratum
(requires ⟨VERIFY-TEMPORAL-TABLES⟩).

**T2 — Channel sensitivity.** *Prediction:* the grounded−stripped effect is
larger on the fabrication and gold-disambiguation channels than on the composite
score. *Threshold:* fabrication-channel effect size ≥ ⟨T2-RATIO⟩ × composite
effect size, same direction. *Failure condition:* if the composite shows the
larger or an equal effect, T2 is false — language did not mask the loss. *Runs
against:* existing clean bare-model dataset; no new execution required.

**T3 — Recovery hysteresis.** Per unit, residual = Grounded − Re-grounded; each
stripped failure classified fabrication-type or uncertainty-type. *Prediction:*
fabrication-type units carry negative residuals (return worn); uncertainty-type
units return whole or better. *Threshold:* sign-agreement between failure type
and residual sign above ⟨T3-FLOOR⟩. *Failure condition:* if residual sign is
uncorrelated with failure type — or if fabrication-type units recover better —
T3 is false. *Runs against:* existing paired data + ratings.

**T4 — Substrate invariance.** The full grounded/stripped/re-grounded protocol
run on a second model (⟨VERIFY-MODEL-2⟩) and a non-vector representation
(⟨VERIFY-GRAPH-ARM⟩). *Prediction:* the seven-category degradation **signature**
(the rank pattern across categories) reappears on both despite different absolute
scores. *Threshold:* cross-substrate signature rank-correlation ≥ ⟨T4-FLOOR⟩.
*Failure condition:* if the signature scrambles across substrate, T4 is false —
and the effect is substrate-dependent, itself a reportable finding. *Runs
against:* new second-model and graph arms.

> T4 is the only testable residue of a stronger structural claim. It tests
> whether grounding-dependence is a property of the **structure** rather than the
> substrate; a positive T4 is evidence for structural kinship and is **not**
> evidence that representational and physical geometry are the same object.

# 21. Situated Meaning Degradation Categories

Seven categories, with fabrication modes scored separately from the composite:

1. **Flattening** — loss of relationships among entities; isolated facts retained
   without their connections.
2. **Temporal Confusion** — incorrect ordering; failure to distinguish historical
   from current; temporal identity drift.
3. **Referential Drift** — switching referents; merging unrelated entities;
   losing continuity.
4. **Disambiguation Loss** — failure to resolve competing meanings; ambiguous
   answers without appropriate uncertainty.
5. **Provenance Incoherence** — unsupported attribution; incorrect source claims;
   loss of evidence boundaries.
6. **Confident Unsupported Provenance Fabrication** — invented source
   attribution, fabricated collection pathways, unsupported institutional claims;
   confident construction of false structure rather than loss of information.
7. **Confident Overwrite of Accurate Ground Truth** — correctly identifying a
   value, asserting it is wrong, and substituting an unsupported one; degradation
   caused by additional opportunity for confident error, not missing information.

Categories 6 and 7 record a finding from prior execution: fabrication correlated
with data *presence*, not absence. This is a hypothesis carried into the clean-
slate run, not an established result.

# 22. Interpretation Framework

The experiment evaluates one proposition — do structured relational constraints
contribute measurably to semantic coherence? — and does not evaluate
consciousness, subjective awareness, quantum cognition, Orch OR, or metaphysical
theories of meaning.

## 22.1 Positive Result

Requires: grounded > stripped; re-grounded recovery; consistent error patterns
across units; acceptable blind-scoring reliability. Supports: *structured
representational relationships contribute materially to semantic stability.*
Does **not** prove: geometry is required for consciousness; artificial systems
have subjective experience; Penrose is correct; semantic vectors are physical
quantum states.

## 22.2 Negative Result

Also informative. Interpretations: symbolic representation preserves meaning
better than hypothesized; the selected grounding dimensions are not constitutive;
the stripping failed to remove the relevant constraints; another mechanism
maintains coherence. Narrows the possible role of geometry-dependent
explanations.

# 23. Data Sovereignty and Fayette County Context

The system was developed around rural data sovereignty, community
representation, local knowledge preservation, and accountable interpretation.
The scientific claim does **not** depend on location: the study does not claim
rural systems uniquely produce intelligence, that Appalachian knowledge is
inherently superior, or that geography creates consciousness. Fayette County
provides an operational environment where relationships among place, community,
data, identity, and governance can be examined. The broader principle: *data
about communities should preserve the relationships that make that data
meaningful* — a record can remain technically accessible while becoming
functionally less understandable once its relational context is lost.

---

# 24. Limitations

**24.1 Structural interpretation.** Geometric terminology (state space, vector
relationship, embedding, geometry) is representational and implies no physical
equivalence with quantum systems. Mathematical structure does not establish
physical similarity.

**24.2 Consciousness.** The protocol measures semantic coherence, referential
stability, contextual reasoning, and grounding dependence — not subjective
experience. Improved semantic performance does not imply consciousness.

**24.3 Orch OR.** A positive result does not validate Orch OR; the experiment
examines no microtubules, quantum collapse, biological cognition, or objective
reduction. The relationship is indirect.

**24.4 Model.** Results may depend on architecture, scale, training data, prompt
design, retrieval mechanism, and evaluation procedure; conclusions are evidence
about the tested systems under defined conditions. (T4 directly probes how far
this generalizes.)

**24.5 Intervention.** Separating structure from semantic content is imperfect:
a token such as "West Virginia" may function simultaneously as a geographic
identifier, cultural reference, semantic token, and retrieval cue. Stripping is a
controlled approximation, not perfect isolation.

**24.6 Human evaluation.** Blind rating still carries interpretation variability;
inter-rater reliability is required before inference.

**24.7 Measurement channel.** The dependent variable is linguistic: behavior is
measured through generated text scored by human raters. Natural language is not a
neutral channel — it is itself a structured relational representation carrying
distributional, syntactic, and co-occurrence relationships independent of the
grounding variables. Consequently the stripped condition does not compare
structured against unstructured representation; it compares two sources of
structure — grounding-derived constraints against constraints recoverable from
linguistic content alone. Two implications: (a) the model may reconstruct
grounding-like information from residual linguistic content even after the
structured variables are removed (expected behavior, not measurement error);
(b) the composite coherence score is the measure most susceptible to this
masking, while fabrication rate, gold-disambiguation, and unsupported-inference
rate are least susceptible and are weighted accordingly (Sections 15, 20.4). This
clarifies what the design measures: the **marginal** contribution of structured
grounding over the relational structure already carried by language.

# 26. Expected Contributions

A methodological framework, not a claim that geometry creates consciousness:

1. a controlled intervention for removing structured constraints;
2. a measurement framework for semantic degradation that separates information
   loss from relational loss;
3. an evaluation model separating uncertainty from fabrication;
4. a set of pre-registered directional predictions each with an explicit failure
   condition, including a substrate-invariance test that distinguishes structural
   from substrate-bound effects;
5. a reproducible pathway for studying grounded intelligence systems.

---

# 27. Broader Theoretical Implication (bounded)

If supported, the results suggest that intelligence systems may require not only
more isolated information but preservation of relationships among information —
consistent with the principle that *information is stored not only in elements
but in relationships*. A database of correct facts can still fail to preserve
meaning if the relationships among them are destroyed: a map without spatial
relationships is a different object, not a smaller one; a record without temporal
relationships encodes a different understanding of events; a provenance record
without lineage changes the ability to evaluate trust. This is a claim about
**structure**, and structure is by nature multiply realizable — which is what T4
tests and, if it holds, what the result demonstrates. No stronger claim — of
substance identity between representational and physical geometry — is made or
supported by this design. (Conjectures beyond this boundary are confined to
Appendix F and generate no predictions in this study.)

---

# 28. Conclusion

This protocol tests whether structured relational grounding contributes
measurably to semantic coherence in an artificial intelligence system. It does
not determine whether machines are conscious, test whether Penrose is correct,
or connect artificial representations to physical spacetime geometry. It examines
whether relationships among representations affect meaning preservation, and it
pre-registers directional predictions such that both positive and null outcomes
are informative.

The central hypothesis remains open:

> Meaning in artificial systems may depend not only on what information is
> present, but on how that information is structured.

# Appendix C — Final Research Position

> Structured relational constraints may contribute to semantic coherence in

# Appendix D — Reproducibility and Provenance

**D.1 Confirmatory vs exploratory.** Confirmatory: research question, primary
hypothesis, three-condition design, inclusion/exclusion, scoring dimensions,
primary comparisons, T1–T4 with frozen thresholds, human-evaluation requirement.
Exploratory: newly observed failure categories, unexpected behaviors,
implementation discoveries. Exploratory findings generate future hypotheses and
are not confirmatory evidence.

**D.2 Data management.** Minimum artifacts: unit definitions, condition-
generation log, prompt manifest, execution results (durable store export), blind
scoring forms, reliability results, analysis script. Each result traces source
unit → condition → execution-path → response → rater judgment → score.

**D.3 Evidence categories.** Category 1 — direct measurement (outputs,
timestamps, scores, rater judgments). Category 2 — system verification (database
inspection, service availability, source-code review, runtime tests). Category
3 — conceptual interpretation (theoretical implications, philosophy, future
hypotheses). Category 3 claims are never presented as Category 1 measurements.

**D.4 Reproducibility.** Document environment (hardware, OS, runtime, model ids,
dependency versions), data (source records, transformations, exclusions),
execution (prompts, parameters, inference settings, capture), and evaluation
(scoring instructions, separated rater identities, reliability calculations).

**D.5 Negative-results policy.** A null result is informative. Outcome A
(grounded > stripped, re-grounded > stripped): structured constraints
contribute. Outcome B (grounded ≈ stripped): the tested constraints do not
materially affect behavior. Outcome C (stripped > grounded): the removed
constraints add noise or bias. Outcome D (mixed): different tasks depend on
different structure.

**D.6 Ethics.** No human subjects as experimental participants; no personal
psychological inference; no claims about machine consciousness or human
cognitive superiority. Community data used for grounding is handled under
applicable privacy requirements, data minimization, provenance documentation,
and responsible-use standards.

# Appendix E — Author Statement of Research Boundary

The central contribution is a testable engineering proposition:

> Information systems may preserve meaning more effectively when relationships
> among information are maintained.

The experiment asks whether location improves interpretation, time improves
continuity, provenance improves trust, and restoring relationships recovers lost
coherence — all measurable. The answers may inform the design of intelligence
systems that are more accountable, interpretable, context-aware, and faithful to
the communities they represent. The result, positive or negative, contributes
evidence toward understanding how artificial systems construct and preserve
meaning.

# Appendix F — Motivation and Conjecture (Not Part of the Confirmatory Protocol)

> This appendix records the conceptual motivations behind the design. **It is
> explicitly not part of the confirmatory protocol and generates no predictions
> tested in this study.** It is retained for intellectual provenance under
> Category 3 (Appendix D.3). Nothing here may be cited as a result of this work.

The design was motivated by a family of relational ideas: that identity may be
constituted by relations rather than intrinsic substance (structural realism in
the philosophy of physics); that meaning, like a coordinate frame, may require
coordinatization to carry relational content; and that temporal structure is
helical, combining a linear arrow with a cyclical return (Section 5.2.1). These
motivations are structural analogies, not physical claims. In particular, the
conjecture that meaning-space and physical space might be "the same manifold" is
**not** advanced as a claim of this study: it lacks a correspondence rule
relating a coordinate of meaning to a specific physical coordinate, it fails
under the multiple realizability of any given meaning across physically disjoint
substrates, and it is empirically inert with respect to this experiment. The
defensible residue of that motivation — that grounding-dependence is a property
of structure rather than substrate — is promoted into the confirmatory protocol
as T4 (Section 20.4) and tested there. The conjecture itself remains, in the
author's terms, a conviction underneath the work rather than a claim inside it.
